# THE ROOM
## Chapter I: The Last Ledger — Story Spine v3

### What this is

A narrative persuasion simulator. Each room is a stand-alone case with its own
person, its own objection and its own tactics, and can be replayed alone. The
conspiracy sits on top of the rooms rather than replacing them. It is not a pure
sandbox simulator, and the design does not claim to be one.

### Story

Antwerp, eleven days after port financier Daniel Voss vanished. The news says he
washed 240 million euros through shipping funds and left his bookkeeper dead in a
locked office. You are Hale: no first name on screen, no face, only your own
hands. Your one fixed fact is a 2001 mountain posting in the Balkans, where four
men swore a brotherhood over a woman you loved and another one of them killed.
Eight rooms, eight people, each wanting a signature, a favour or a silence. It
starts small, with a wife's tidy lie in a kitchen, a bank clerk who says the file
was never opened, a lawyer offering a deal that sounds like kindness. Each
conversation turns up one true piece, and the pieces point back at the posting.
One brother is selling the others to a cartel that has spent years inside the
port. One is protecting the killing. One is dying and wants it said. By the War
Room you learn the bookkeeper did not die because of Voss. He died because of a
line you said in the second room, and the voice steering you from the start was
Voss, who plays your own words back at you in the Mirror and asks what you are.
Every room holds a clock that runs four minutes slow.

### Fixed design decisions

- Title: THE ROOM. Chapter I is The Last Ledger. No other subtitle.
- Hale is a role, not a character. Hands visible in first-person shots. The 2001
  scar is on the back of your hand, and you touch it before the Mirror.
- CALL stays and is the main Insight move: click a tell, name the tactic, the
  tactic weakens x0.25 for that room. A wrong CALL costs 10 trust.
- The 2001 posting is planted from Room 1: the photograph on the fridge, a name
  the clerk recognises in Room 2, the scar.
- Soul Sheets are written before any dialogue. Every NPC gets a want, a
  contradiction and one unexpected human moment.
- War Room keeps its content note and skip.

### The score

Three visible parts, all shown on the end screen.

| Part | Earned by |
|---|---|
| Insight | correct CALLs, hidden objections uncovered, fake time pressure spotted |
| Craft | clean moves that landed (truthful and refusal-preserving) |
| Edge | skull moves that worked and were not detected |

Some moments carry no score at all: a kindness, a silence, a refusal to answer.
The end screen states that not everything was measured.

### The clock

| Behaviour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reads four minutes slow in every room | continuity, and a tell |
| NPC cites the clock to rush a decision | the pressure is manufactured; CALL it for Insight |
| Loses more time as you near the truth | pacing signal, no score effect |
| Stops at the end of Room 8, only if you gave the Mirror nothing | you left on your own terms |

### The eight rooms

| # | Room | NPC | The ask | Skill | Truth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kitchen | Anna Voss | "He was home that night." | specific ask, reciprocity, probing | the photograph: four men, 2001 |
| 2 | Desk | the clerk | "The file was never opened." | framing inside constraints | it was opened; your careless line here plants the bookkeeper's death |
| 3 | Table | the lawyer | "Sign. It's kindness." | acknowledge feeling before logic | the deal is a silencer |
| 4 | Deposit | the tenant | "I don't remember." | documentation, escalation ladder | the flat where Voss hid |
| 5 | Meeting | the board | "One name. Not ours." | detect flattery, triangulation, DARVO | the board wants a scapegoat |
| 6 | Fund | the investors | "We don't care whose money it is." | non-committal answers, anchoring | the trail runs to the cartel |
| 7 | War Room | Marek, De Vries, the dying brother | "One of us is selling. One of us killed her." | grey rock, evidence, exit planning | the 2001 killing, and your Room 2 line |
| 8 | Mirror | Voss | "What are you?" | hold a calm, repeated no | every tactic you used, in your own words |

### Room outcomes

Each room resolves clean, partial, dark or loss, at medium and hard. A dark win
carries a delayed cost into a later room.

| Room | Clean | Partial | Dark | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Kitchen | lie exposed, she trusts you | half-truth, photograph missed | she signs a false alibi you now own | she shuts the door |
| 2 Desk | file opened, nobody burned | file opened, clerk exposed | clerk silenced, bookkeeper debt begins | you are walked out |
| 3 Table | refuse, keep options | partial deal | you sign (Signature path) | frozen out |
| 4 Deposit | he talks by choice | he talks, frightened | bought silence, he disappears | evicted, trail cold |
| 5 Meeting | no name given | a middleman named | a brother named | you are named |
| 6 Fund | money frozen clean | partial freeze | you take it, trust gone | locked out |
| 7 War Room | the truth said openly | truth, one of them pays | one sold out | brotherhood breaks |
| 8 Mirror | gave it nothing, clock stops | cracked, not free | fed it (Chair path) | fed it, named |

### The Mirror rule

The Mirror wins if you hand it new material. It loses if you hand it nothing. A
calm repeated boundary is the strongest version of that, not the only one. A
silence, a direct question or a piece of evidence all hold, as long as none of
them feeds it a new reason to work with.

Dying Brother, Open Ledger, Silence, Wall, Scapegoat, Signature.

### How the top three end

**The Double Cross.** You out-thought all of them and kept one lie straight
across three rooms. The last beat is Voss setting down a folder with every one of
your moves written in it by date. He is not angry and he does not stop you. He
says the seat is yours because he picked you in Room 1, and that the only person
you did not read was him. You take the chair knowing it was handed to you. One
missed CALL, one overplayed line or one contradiction drops you to the Chair or
the Scapegoat instead.

**The Chair.** No music, no sound effect and no text. One image: the kitchen at
11 p.m., a stranger sitting down. Then black. The same image the game opened on.
No congratulation anywhere.

**The Cartel's Man.** You are useful and you are kept. The last screen is a port
at night and a phone that rings whenever they want it to.
