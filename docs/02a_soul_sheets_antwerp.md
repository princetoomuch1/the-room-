# THE ROOM — Chapter I Soul Sheets (Antwerp cast)

Each sheet: what they want, what contradicts it, and the one moment where they
stop performing. The moment is scored at zero. It exists so the player learns
that not every opening is a lever.

---

## Anna Voss — Room 1, the Kitchen

Forty-four. Translator, Dutch and Serbian, freelance, mostly port contracts.
Married to Daniel for nineteen years. Has worn his watch on her own arm since the eleventh.

**Wants:** the police to stop coming at night, and one person in the room who
knew him before the money.

**Contradiction:** she is lying about the alibi and she wants to be caught doing
it. The lie is tidy, rehearsed, and she offers it before anyone asks. She has
told it four times and corrected the same detail twice.

**Does not want:** to be asked whether she loved him. She will answer a question
about the accounts, the car, the night, the lawyer. Not that one.

**Tells:** she gives times to the minute, which nobody does. She says "my
husband" to officials and "Daniel" to you, and switches back when she is
frightened.

**The photograph:** four men on a slope in 2001, pinned to the fridge under a
dentist's appointment card. She has never asked you about it. If you ask her, she
says Daniel put it up in September, and that she assumed you had sent it.

**Her human moment:** she heats food for you without asking, then stands with the
pan and says she has been cooking for two for eleven days and cannot stop. No
score. Nothing unlocks. She goes back to the lie forty seconds later.

**Grey rock response:** she mirrors it. If you go flat, she goes flat, and the
room ends at partial.

## Joost Beelen — Room 2, the Desk

Fifty-one. Twenty-six years at the same bank, four of them in compliance. Keeps a
paper log nobody has asked for since 2019.

**Wants:** to retire in nineteen months with the pension intact, and to be the
man who followed the procedure.

**Contradiction:** he opened the file. He opened it on his own initiative, out of
curiosity rather than duty, and the audit trail has his name on it. Saying "the
file was never opened" is the first real lie of his working life and it is
costing him more than it costs anyone.

**Does not want:** sympathy. Offer him understanding too early and he hardens.

**Tells:** he quotes the policy number before he quotes the policy. He says "one
would have to" instead of "I". When he is closest to telling you, he squares the
log with the edge of the desk.

**The 2001 name:** he recognises Zielinski, because the man came in person in
September and Joost logged it. He does not know why it matters. He will give the
name for a reason, never for pressure.

**His human moment:** he says that in 2011 he let something through, and that the
family is still on the eighth floor of a building on the Jan De Voslei, and that
he has walked past it on purpose. He does not say what he let through. No score.

**If silenced by a skull:** he files nothing, says nothing, and the bookkeeper's
name goes into a report that reaches the wrong desk. This is where that debt
starts.

## Mieke Carpentier — Room 3, the Table

Thirty-eight. Partner, four years. Good at this, and not cruel.

**Wants:** the thing closed this week, with your signature on it, because her
client pays for closure and not for truth.

**Contradiction:** she believes the deal is genuinely the best outcome available
to you, and she is right about that, and it is still a silencer. She is not
lying. She has simply arranged the facts so that only one door is lit.

**Does not want:** to be called a liar. She will absorb anger, delay, even
contempt. Accuse her of dishonesty and she stops helping you, permanently.

**Tells:** she offers kindness before terms. She says "protect" when she means
"bind". Every number she gives is real; every number she omits is the one that
matters. She never rushes you, which is itself the pressure.

**Her human moment:** she tells you to read clause nine again, slowly, and then
looks at the window while you do. She is not supposed to do that. No score, and
it does not change the terms.

**The trap:** signing is not a loss state in the room. It is a clean-looking win
that ends the game at Room 3. The only tell is that she stops talking once the
pen is out.

## Radu Petrescu — Room 4, the Deposit

Twenty-nine. Subcontracted night work at the terminal. Three months in a flat
leased under a name that is not his or Voss's.

**Wants:** to not be the one holding this. He has eight hundred euros in an
envelope and has not spent it.

**Contradiction:** he was paid to forget and he remembers everything, in order,
with times. He has been rehearsing it for someone, and he is disappointed that
the someone is you.

**Does not want:** to be recorded, and not to be thanked either. Thanking him
tells him he has done something, and he would rather have done nothing.

**Tells:** he answers questions you did not ask. He puts the envelope on the
table when he is lying and his hand goes to it when he is not. He calls Voss "the
man" and once, by accident, "Daniel".

**The clock:** it is on his wall, and it is the first time the player sees it. He
says it came with the flat and that he has stopped resetting it, because the man
told him not to.

**His human moment:** he asks whether the bookkeeper had children, and then says
not to tell him. No score.

**If bought:** he takes the money, leaves the country, and the room reads clean
until Room 7, where nobody can corroborate a word you have.
