# The Kitchen — painter's brief

**Medium.** Oil on canvas, visible weave, craquelure at the frame edge. The paint
sits thicker where the light is. Nothing in the frame is rendered or
photographic; every edge is a brush edge.

**Light.** One bare overhead bulb, slightly green, filament visible. Hard
shadows straight down. Everything past a metre from the bulb falls to near-black.
The window over the sink is dark: it is 11 p.m. and raining, and the rain exists
only as streaks catching the bulb.

**Palette (five).** Bone white for the tiles. Burnt sienna for the counter edge.
Oxide red for the tea towel. Arsenic green for the bulb's cast on the wall. One
gold: a wedding ring on the counter, not on a finger.

**Composition.** Sink centre-left, full, the water gone cold, one plate still
has food on it. Anna stands right of centre, back half-turned, one hand flat on
the counter, the other holding a cigarette she is not smoking. She is not looking
at you. The fridge is behind her, and on the fridge a photograph under a magnet:
four men, faces not readable, the light does not reach it.

**The wrong object.** A second cup, clean, dry, upside down. Someone else sat
here. She has not mentioned it. It costs insight to examine and tells you nothing
about the night, only about the visit in September.

**The clock.** Above the door, four minutes slow, ticking. It is not a timer.

**Movement.** The bulb flickers once every twenty seconds. The rain moves. The
smoke curls. Her eyes blink. Nothing else moves unless she moves.
