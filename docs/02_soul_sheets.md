# Soul Sheets

Twenty-six people. Twenty-one archetype NPCs (the Mirror has a construction rule, not a soul) and the five people of the War Room. Fields follow the ten-field standard in the bible §2, plus the **soul moment** and a one-line **portrait** note for the painter.

Endings key: **Clean** = clean agreement · **Partial** · **Dark** = dark compliance · **Walk** = refusal or walk-out.

---

## 1. Hemant Joshi: the Staller (Room 1, The Kitchen)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for him.** Hemant Vasant Joshi, 52. Grew up in Kasba Peth, Pune, in the same building he lives in now, two floors up. His mother called him *Hemu*, and he calls himself Hemu when he is caught out: "Hemu, you fool."

**Work.** Owns Joshi Xerox & Stationery opposite Fergusson College gate. Opens 8:30, closes 9:30, Sundays half-day. Lamination, spiral binding, exam-season photocopies, and a side line in printer cartridges that he refills himself.

**What he loves.** The 6:15 a.m. walk up Parvati hill, every day since 1991, counting the steps (103 to the first landing). He tells you the number, unprompted, while you are trying to discuss a bank form.

**Contradiction.** He believes he is the dependable one in the family, the uncle who turns up. He does turn up. He does not finish things, and he has never once told anyone why.

**Wound.** In 2014 he stood guarantor for a friend's loan and paid it off himself over six years, and he told nobody, including his wife.

**What he wants from you.** To be taken seriously. Not to be let off. He wants someone to ask him what is wrong as if he were a man with a business and not an uncle with a tumbler.

**How he speaks.**
- "One minute, one minute. First tell me, you've eaten? There's poha from morning."
- "These bank people, they think everybody is a criminal until the paper says otherwise."
- "A hundred and three steps. I don't miss. Even the Covid time, I went at five so no police."

**What he notices about you.** Whether you finish your tea. If you leave it, he thinks you are in a hurry to get away from him.

**What he will never say.** "I don't have the money." He will say anything else first.

**Soul moment.** Mid-way through the Kitchen, he tells a joke about the bank manager's moustache, laughs at it, stops, and says: "Sorry. That wasn't funny. I'm tired." Then he asks if you want more poha.

**Portrait.** Short, round-shouldered, half-sleeve shirt with a ballpoint clipped to the pocket; thumb working the rim of a steel tumbler.

**Endings.**
- **Clean.** Hemant goes to the bank with you on Saturday in his good shirt and argues with the loans officer about the fixed-deposit lien for forty minutes and wins. That evening he tells his wife about the 2014 loan. She is angry for a week. Then, on his birthday, she gives him a pedometer, and he says he doesn't need it, he counts, and wears it anyway.
- **Partial.** The file is rejected on Monday. He says the bank changed its rules. He believes it by Wednesday. He does not offer again, and you do not ask him again, and at family weddings you both talk about the weather in Pune as if it were a serious subject.
- **Dark.** He withdraws as guarantor by letter, two weeks later, typed on the shop's machine. He tells your mother you spoke to him "like a recovery agent." He still walks up Parvati every morning. He has started stopping at the first landing.
- **Walk.** You leave without the signature. He finishes the dishes after you go, every one, and puts the tumbler back on the shelf. In the morning he walks the hill. He loses count at sixty and starts again.

## 2. Gillian Pryce: the Bureaucrat (Room 2, The Desk)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for her.** Gillian Margaret Pryce, 58. Grew up in Stockport, two-up two-down, father on the buses. Her mother called her *Gilly*. When flustered she mutters "come on, Gilly" at the screen.

**Work.** Student Records Officer, Grade 5, at a university in Manchester. Nineteen years on the same desk. Monday to Friday, 8:45 to 4:45, lunch at 12:30 exactly, in the staff room, with a Tupperware of the same pasta salad.

**What she loves.** Crown green bowls at the Heaton Mersey club on Wednesday evenings. She has a set of Drakes Pride woods she bought in 1999. If you mention anything about weight or bias or a slope, she lights up.

**Contradiction.** She believes the rules protect students. They do. She also hides behind them, because the one time she stepped out from behind them, she was the one who got hurt.

**Wound.** Last spring she released a transcript with a hold on it, for a student whose father was dying abroad, and was formally reprimanded in writing.

**What she wants from you.** To be asked inside the rules, so that she can help you without being the one who bends.

**How she speaks.**
- "I'm not being funny, love, but if I click that button, my name's on it, not yours."
- "There's a form for that. There's a form for everything. That's the point of forms."
- "You've got to read the green. Everyone thinks it's flat. It's never flat."

**What she notices about you.** Whether you read the laminated sign before speaking. Most people don't.

**What she will never say.** "I made a mistake." She will say "there was an issue" and "it was dealt with".

**Soul moment.** While the system loads, she offers you a Werther's Original from a tin in her drawer, and when you take one she says, "My dad always had them on the bus. For the kids who cried."

**Portrait.** Cardigan in heather green with one button sewn on in a different thread; reading glasses on a beaded chain; a date stamp at her right hand.

**Endings.**
- **Clean.** She walks the emergency release form upstairs herself. At her grade review in June, her manager cites "excellent judgement within procedure, e.g. the registrar's release in March." She wins the club's spring singles. She doesn't tell anyone at work, but she brings in a tin of biscuits.
- **Partial.** Your transcript arrives a day late. She never knows whether it mattered. She thinks about you once, in July, when a similar case comes up, and does it faster.
- **Dark.** She files an incident report the same afternoon, four pages, single-spaced. She passes her grade review. She stops offering the sweets to students. The tin stays in the drawer.
- **Walk.** She calls the next ticket. At 4:45 she switches off the banker's lamp and sits for a moment in the dark office before she goes, which she has never done before.

## 3. Ramesh Deshpande: the Wounded King (Room 3, The Table)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for him.** Ramesh Govind Deshpande, 63, your father. Grew up in Satara; moved to Pune at seventeen for college. His mother called him *Ramu*. He says it once, to himself, at the sink: "Ramu, enough."

**Work.** Chartered accountant. R. G. Deshpande & Co., two rooms above a sweet shop on Tilak Road. Thirty-four years. Eleven clients who have been with him since the eighties and whom he still calls "sir."

**What he loves.** Old Marathi *natya sangeet* records, specifically Bal Gandharva, on a cassette player he has repaired four times. He hums while correcting other people's balance sheets.

**Contradiction.** He believes he never forced his child into anything. He has been forcing gently, every day, for twenty years, by assuming.

**Wound.** His own father took his engineering admission letter from the post and replaced it with an accountancy form, and he never fought it.

**What he wants from you.** An audience who will not leave. Failing that, a reason to believe the practice was worth building even if no one takes it over.

**How he speaks.**
- "London, London. Fine. The Queen is dead, you know. Who will you work for, the King?"
- "In 1997 the flood came to the first step of the office. I carried the files up myself. Your mother carried you."
- "Eat. You're thin. They don't have proper food there, only sandwiches."

**What he notices about you.** Whether you look at the photograph on the sideboard. You don't know yet that he turned it face-down.

**What he will never say.** "I was wrong." He has said "maybe I was not completely right" twice in his life.

**Soul moment.** After the main argument, with nothing resolved, he gets up, fetches a second helping of shrikhand you didn't ask for, puts it on your plate, and sits down again without a word.

**Portrait.** Thin, white vest under a checked shirt, reading glasses pushed up into grey hair; one hand flat on the tablecloth as if holding it down.

**Endings.**
- **Clean.** He becomes, without announcement, your tax adviser in two countries. He sends you the HMRC link at 2 a.m. and a voice note explaining it for eleven minutes. He turns the photograph of his father face-up again in November. He plays Bal Gandharva in the office with the door open for the first time.
- **Partial.** He comes to the airport and carries one bag. He calls on your birthday and asks if you've eaten. He does not ask anything else for a year.
- **Dark.** He does not call for four months. When he does, it is to say that Joglekar's son has joined the practice. He keeps the photograph face-down. When you visit next Diwali, there is a second name painted on the board outside the office, and it is not yours.
- **Walk.** He goes to bed at 8:40 and your mother finishes dinner in silence. In the morning he has left for the office before you are awake. On the table there is a single folded sheet: the address of a CA in Croydon who "knows NRI rules." No note.

## 4. Nabil Haddad: the Closer, landlord variant (Room 4, The Deposit)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for him.** Nabil Fouad Haddad, 49. Born in Tripoli, Lebanon; came to Manchester at fourteen in 1991. His mother called him *Billo*. When he is losing an argument he says, half to himself, "Yalla, Billo."

**Work.** Landlord. Owns, with his brother Karim, a building of six flats above shops in Rusholme, plus a phone-repair shop on the ground floor he runs himself, 10 to 8, six days.

**What he loves.** Making ka'ak bread on Sunday mornings with his daughters, badly, in a domestic oven that can't get hot enough. He shows you a photo on his phone of a burnt one. Unprompted.

**Contradiction.** He believes he is a fair man who is too soft with tenants. He is soft with tenants he likes, and he decides which ones he likes in the first two minutes.

**Wound.** In 2008 a tenant took him to court over a boiler and he lost, and the judgement was read out in front of his father.

**What he wants from you.** To leave as a decent person he can describe to his brother as a decent person, with the scheme never mentioned.

**How he speaks.**
- "Look. Look at me. I am not a bad man. I am a man with a carpet."
- "Today, cash, finish. Tomorrow I'm in Beirut, you're in London, everybody is sad."
- "My oven, it's English oven. It thinks two hundred is hot. Two hundred is nothing."

**What he notices about you.** Whether you check the door when you come in. People who check the door know someone is behind them.

**What he will never say.** The name of the deposit scheme.

**Soul moment.** His phone rings; it is his younger daughter. He answers in Arabic, soft, and says "Habibti, I'm in a meeting, Baba's in a meeting," and laughs, and hangs up, and looks at you as if he had forgotten you were there.

**Portrait.** Big man in a tight polo shirt, gold chain, reading glasses he won't wear; fingers drumming on a stack of receipts.

**Endings.**
- **Clean.** He returns £900. That Sunday the ka'ak comes out properly for the first time, because Karim buys him an oven thermometer for no reason. He registers the next tenant's deposit within a week, and tells Karim it was his own idea.
- **Partial.** He keeps £350 he wasn't owed. He tells himself it was for the carpet. In March he replaces the carpet and it costs £180.
- **Dark.** Karim learns through the council. The brothers stop speaking for a year. Nabil sells his half of the building to Karim at a loss and keeps the shop. On Sundays he still makes the bread. It's still burnt.
- **Walk.** You take the £350. He counts it out on the desk, twice. When you leave he puts the rest of your deposit in the drawer that sticks, and the drawer sticks, and he kicks it, and it opens.

## 5. Olivia Marsh: the Charmer (Room 5, The Meeting)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for her.** Olivia Jane Marsh, 36. Grew up in Hove; father a dentist, mother a magistrate. Her mother called her *Livvy*, and she signs internal Slack messages "Liv" except when she is frightened, when she signs them "Olivia."

**Work.** Head of Product at a forecasting software company in Farringdon. Nominally 9 to 6. Actually 7:30 to whenever, plus Sunday-night email.

**What she loves.** Wild swimming at the Hampstead ladies' pond, all year round, including January. She brings it up in the Meeting to explain why she is "basically unflappable."

**Contradiction.** She believes she is generous with credit. She is generous with *praise*, which costs her nothing, and careful with credit, which does.

**Wound.** At her first job she was the one whose work was presented by a manager, and she said nothing, and he was promoted.

**What she wants from you.** For you to like her, specifically, and to make this go away without her having to choose between you and her promise to the board.

**How she speaks.**
- "Okay, I love this. I love that you care this much. Can I be really honest with you for a sec?"
- "The pond was four degrees on Sunday. Four! You just have to decide not to have an opinion about it."
- "Nobody's saying you didn't do brilliant work. Literally nobody."

**What she notices about you.** Whether you laugh at her jokes in the first minute. If you don't, she works harder.

**What she will never say.** "I promised Tom." She will say "we all agreed" first.

**Soul moment.** Her phone lights up with a calendar alert: "Mum, oncology, 3pm." She turns it over quickly, and for a second her face is not doing anything at all, and then it is again.

**Portrait.** Linen blazer, sleeves pushed up, many thin rings; hands always moving (pen, ring, hair, the table's edge).

**Endings.**
- **Clean.** Friday goes well. In the debrief she tells Richard you're "sharp, a bit stubborn, keep them." In her next one-to-one with Tom she says, for the first time, "I don't think you're ready yet, and that's on me." She swims on Sunday. The water is three degrees.
- **Partial.** Your name is in small type on slide six. She sends you a gif of a trophy. She means it. It still isn't what you asked for.
- **Dark.** She moves you to a project nobody wants. She is never unkind. In a reference two years later she writes "highly capable" and nothing else, and the recruiter knows exactly what that means.
- **Walk.** You leave Kew. She sits in the empty room and writes "Talk to Tom" on the whiteboard, under Monday's agenda, and then rubs it out with her sleeve.

## 6. Tom Whitcombe: the DARVO Defender (Room 5, The Meeting)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for him.** Thomas Edward Whitcombe, 41. Grew up in Guildford, private school on a bursary he never mentions. His mother called him *Tommo*. He calls himself Tommo in his head when he gets something wrong.

**Work.** Senior Project Lead, same company. Manages four people. Promoted twice in five years, mostly on presentation skills.

**What he loves.** His allotment in Walthamstow, where he grows chillies (he has nineteen varieties). He offers you a Scotch bonnet from a paper bag in the meeting.

**Contradiction.** He believes he is a team player who gets thrown under the bus. He is a man who does not understand the model and has decided it is safer to be wronged than ignorant.

**Wound.** He failed his first-year maths exams at university and resat them in secret over the summer and never told his parents.

**What he wants from you.** Not to be found out on Friday.

**How he speaks.**
- "I don't know what you think happened here. I genuinely don't."
- "After everything I did in sprint review? I covered for you, and this is what I get?"
- "That's a Carolina Reaper cross. Don't. Seriously. Unless you hate yourself."

**What he notices about you.** Whether you use the technical terms. Every time you do, he gets quieter.

**What he will never say.** "I don't understand it."

**Soul moment.** When you mention the model's Bayesian prior, he nods, and then, too quietly for Olivia to hear, says, "I'll be honest, I've never actually got that bit," and immediately coughs as if he hadn't.

**Portrait.** Quarter-zip fleece with company logo, a slightly too-big watch; a paper bag of chillies on the table in front of him.

**Endings.**
- **Clean.** He presents the business case on Friday and you present the method. When the board asks the hard question he says, "That's for my colleague," and it is the best moment of his career. In spring he asks you to teach him the prior. He brings chillies.
- **Partial.** "With support from" stays on the slide. He thanks you in the all-hands, generously, vaguely. He still doesn't understand the model and the next one breaks in his hands.
- **Dark.** He is moved off the project. He posts a long, wounded LinkedIn update about "resilience after betrayal." It gets four hundred likes. He leaves within the year for a company where nobody knows.
- **Walk.** You leave. He stays in Kew with Olivia and says, "See? I told you they'd be difficult," and she doesn't answer, and he hears that.

## 7. Kwame Asante-Boateng: the Performer (Room 6, The Client)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for him.** Kwame Ofori Asante-Boateng, 50. Born in Kumasi, raised from nine in Harlesden. **Contradiction.** He believes he shouts because he cares. He shouts because it has worked for twenty years, and the shouting started the year he nearly lost everything.

**Wound.** In 2009 a bank pulled his overdraft overnight and he paid his drivers with his wife's jewellery.

**What he wants from you.** Time. He cannot pay this quarter and cannot say so to a supplier, because saying so is how 2009 started.

**How he speaks.**

## 8. Dev Malhotra: the Chess Player (Room 7, The Fund)

**Name, age, origin, mother's name for him.** Devendra Malhotra, 55. Born in Delhi, Lajpat Nagar; moved to London at twenty-three for an MBA. His mother called him *Devu*. He has not heard it since 2016. He says it in the room once, when he mentions her, as if quoting someone else.

**Work.** Managing Partner, Halcyon Growth Partners, a £400m fund in the City. Days start at 6:00 with the FT on paper.

**What he loves.** Squash, Tuesdays and Thursdays at 7 a.m., with the same three people for nineteen years. He knows exactly who you'd beat.

**Contradiction.** He believes he is a rational actor without sentiment. His most important decisions this year have been about his daughter.

**Wound.** He let his first marriage end because he was in Singapore closing a deal on the night his then-wife was in hospital.

**What he wants from you.** A deal before year-end, and to know whether you are someone his daughter could safely work beside.

**How he speaks.**

**What he notices about you.** What you do with your hands. Still hands, he trusts. Busy hands, he waits.

**What he will never say.** "I need this deal."

**Soul moment.** His phone buzzes; the wallpaper is a young woman in a graduation gown. He glances at it and says, mostly to himself, "She's interviewing today," and then, "Sorry. Where were we," and for one second his hands move.

- **Walk.** You walk. He calls back a week later and you are both very polite and the deal is the same deal. He plays squash that Thursday and loses to Ranjan for the first time in four years.

## 9. Richard Haldane: the Cold Operator (Room 8, The Board)
