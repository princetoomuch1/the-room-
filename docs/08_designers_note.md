# A note to the team

**What this game is for.** It is for the moment, months after someone plays it, when a person across a table says "everyone else has already agreed," and the player hears it for what it is, and doesn't have to win anything to be free of it. That's the whole product. Not a power fantasy, not a manipulation tutorial: practice at recognising pressure and staying yourself under it.

**What it refuses to do.**

- It refuses to make cruelty look like competence. Skull moves work, because they do in life, and then they cost what they cost in life: trust, reputation, people who stop calling. We show the cost. We never preach about it.
- It refuses to turn people into locks. Every NPC has a soul moment that scores nothing. If a playtester says "I found the trick for Priya," the room has failed and we rewrite it.
- It refuses to teach folk psychology. No pronoun-counting narcissists, no fearless psychopaths, no evil geniuses, no willpower meters. If a mechanic isn't in the dossier, or the dossier grades it Weak, we don't build a lesson on it.
- It refuses to touch method. No self-harm or suicide method information anywhere, ever, in any file. The crisis corridor and the War Room are written to recognition level only, with support on screen. The lint is a floor, not the standard; the standard is that nobody on this team would be ashamed of a line if a grieving family read it.
- It refuses to end on a lecture. The endings show. The Architect describes. If you catch yourself writing a sentence that tells the player how to feel, cut it.

**How to judge your own work.** Before a room ships, play it three ways: clean, dirty, and badly. Then ask:

1. Did the dirty path win something real, and lose something real later?
2. Could a player beat this room by naming a tactic rather than using one?
3. Did the clean path require probing for what was underneath?
4. Is there a moment where the NPC is simply a person?
5. Would the room's last line be just as true without the Architect saying it?

If any answer is no, the room isn't done.

**Every room must earn one sentence:**

> Dirty tactics win rounds and lose games; recognition beats retaliation; a calm, repeated no beats the best argument.
