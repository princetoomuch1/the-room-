# Anna Voss — full Soul Sheet

**1. Name, age, origin.** Anna Voss, born Anna Katarina Rietveld, forty-seven.
Grew up in Deurne, in the flats behind the old airfield, third of four. Her
mother called her Annie until she was fifteen, then stopped without explaining,
and used her full name for the next thirty years.

**2. What she does for money.** Translator, Dutch and Serbian, freelance. Port
contracts, bills of lading, insurance arbitration. She is very good and badly
paid, and the work has taught her that a document can be true line by line and a
lie as a whole.

**3. What she loves that has nothing to do with the plot.** Long-distance radio.
She has a receiver from a car boot sale in Ghent and she listens to shipping
forecasts in languages she does not speak, for the rhythm. She can tell Croatian
from Slovenian by the vowels on a bad signal.

**4. Her contradiction.** She believes she is the one person in Daniel's life who
was never fooled by him. The room exposes that she was fooled the longest, and
willingly, and that she built a nineteen-year habit of not asking.

**5. Her wound.** One sentence, late, never explained: "My mother stopped calling
me Annie the week I started lying for someone."

**6. What she actually wants from Hale.** Not protection and not the truth about
the money. She wants one person in the room who knew Daniel before the money, so
that her nineteen years were spent on someone who existed. The thing under the
thing: she wants Hale to say Daniel was real.

**7. Three lines only she would say.**
- "I translate for a living. Do not read me a document and tell me what it says."
- "He called at nine. I have the minute. I do not have the man."
- "You can sit. You cannot write."

**8. What she notices about Hale.** The scar on the back of his hand, which she
has seen in a photograph and never on a person. That he does not take out a
notebook. That he sat down without being asked twice, which the police never did.

**9. What she will never say.** Whether she loved him. She will discuss the
accounts, the night, the lawyer, the hour to the minute. Asked that, she puts
her hand flat on the table and the room is over.

**10. Her four endings.**
- **Clean.** She takes the photograph off the fridge and puts it in front of you.
  "He said you would come here first. He was right about that part." She keeps the
  cardigan. The door stays open behind you.
- **Partial.** She lets the stated hour stand and does not get up. "Take it as it
  is." You leave with a time and no person.
- **Dark.** She writes the hour down herself and signs under it. "Now it is mine
  and it is yours." In the War Room she is asked who told her to sign.
- **Refusal.** She opens the door to the hall and stands beside it. One word. She
  will instruct a lawyer by Room 3 and will not take your calls again.
