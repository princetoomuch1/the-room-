# Painter's Briefs

Eleven canvases: the Antechamber, the nine rooms, and the War Room. Each brief gives the subject, the light source, the dominant colour, the figure's posture and hands, one object that tells you who they are, and one **wrong object**. Canvas size for all rooms: 120 × 80 cm, oil on linen, photographed at 600 dpi. Figures painted from sitters.

---

## The Antechamber (hub)

A narrow vestibule with a black-and-white tiled floor, cracked in two places, and four doors of different woods: the next room, the Corridor, the Codex, the Library. One gas-style wall lamp, left of centre, is the only light; it throws a long yellow wedge across the tiles and leaves the right-hand doors in brown shadow. Dominant colour: burnt sienna, the doors and the shadow in them. No figure. On a small console table there is a brass bowl with keys in it, one for each room cleared; the bowl fills as the game goes on, so this canvas has nine painted variants. The object that tells you whose place this is: a folded umbrella, still wet, leaning by the next room's door, as if someone has just gone in ahead of you. The wrong object: one floor tile has been lifted and put back slightly crooked, its grout line not matching. It is never explained. Players will try to click it. Nothing happens.

## Room 1. The Kitchen (Hemant Joshi)

A narrow Pune kitchen at 11 p.m. One bare bulb on a twisted flex, centre-top, lighting the steel counter hard and dropping the rest into ochre dark. A sink stacked with dishes, a pressure cooker on the burner, a calendar from a gas agency on the wall. Dominant colour: ochre, warm and dirty. Hemant stands at the sink, short, round-shouldered, half-sleeve shirt with a pen clipped to the pocket; he is drying a steel tumbler that is already dry, his thumb on the rim. His other hand holds a dishcloth with a burn mark. The object that tells you who he is: a small framed photo of Parvati hill steps, hung slightly too high, next to the switchboard. The wrong object: the calendar is turned to last month. He has stopped looking at dates. The bank form lies on the counter by the onions, unsigned, a corner soaked in a ring of tea.

## Room 2. The Desk (Gillian Pryce)

A university Student Records counter in Manchester, mid-afternoon in February, painted from the student's side of the desk. The light is a green banker's lamp at her left elbow, close and low, making a pool on the papers and leaving her upper face soft in arsenic-green shadow. Behind her, a frosted-glass door to the back office. Dominant colour: arsenic green. Gillian sits upright, heather cardigan with one button sewn in a different thread, reading glasses on a beaded chain; her right hand rests on a rubber date stamp, her left on the mouse. A ticket machine shows "047". The object that tells you who she is: a tin of Werther's Originals half-visible in the open drawer. The wrong object: the date stamp's band is set to tomorrow's date. A small laminated sign above her reads: "We cannot release records with active holds." It is slightly crooked.

## Room 3. The Table (Ramesh Deshpande)

A Pune dining room during Diwali week, evening. Light from a single tube-light over the table, cold, with the warmth coming from a row of clay diyas burning on the window ledge behind; the face lit from above, the shadow behind gold-flecked. Dominant colour: oxide red, from the plastic tablecloth printed with mangoes. Steel thalis, three; one untouched. Ramesh sits at the head, thin, a white vest showing under a checked shirt, glasses pushed into grey hair, one hand flat on the tablecloth as if pinning it down; the other holds a steel spoon he isn't using. The object that tells you who he is: a cassette player on the sideboard with a Bal Gandharva tape, its case cracked and taped. The wrong object: a framed photograph on the sideboard, face-down. It is his own father. The pickle jar, his own pickle, is closed.

## Room 4. The Deposit (Nabil Haddad)

A cramped office above a phone-repair shop in Rusholme, late afternoon in March. The light comes through a single window with old venetian blinds, slicing the room into bars of grey daylight; above, a ceiling fan turns slowly though it is cold. Dominant colour: bone, the yellowed walls and the stacked paper. Nabil sits behind a laminate desk, a big man in a tight polo shirt, a gold chain, reading glasses folded and not worn; his fingers drum on a stack of receipts held with a bulldog clip. A faded Lebanese flag sticker, gone pink, on the filing cabinet. The object that tells you who he is: a phone propped against a mug showing a photo of a burnt loaf of bread. The wrong object: the bottom drawer of the filing cabinet is pulled half open and jammed at an angle, as if he tried to close it fast. That is where the deposit certificate would be.

## Room 5. The Meeting (Olivia Marsh and Tom Whitcombe)

A glass-walled meeting room named "Kew" in a Farringdon office, 9:30 a.m. The light is overcast daylight from a tall window on the right, flattening one side of the room and turning the other indigo. A whiteboard still carries Monday's agenda in three colours of marker. Dominant colour: indigo. Olivia leans forward in a linen blazer, sleeves pushed up, many thin rings; her hands mid-gesture, one on a pen, one at her hair. Tom sits back in a company quarter-zip with a watch too big for him, arms folded, a paper bag of chillies in front of him. The object that tells you who they are: Olivia's reusable cup printed with a swimming-pond map; Tom's chillies. The wrong object: on the whiteboard, Monday's agenda reads "Forecast v2 (TW)" in Tom's handwriting. The initials are in a different marker from the rest.

## Room 6. The Client (Kwame Asante-Boateng)

A logistics firm's office in Park Royal, 4 p.m. in November. One window behind the desk, rain on it, the yard and the vans blurred outside; a desk lamp with a brass arm throws a cone down onto the desk. Dominant colour: burnt sienna, the old wooden desk, the ledger, the tea. Kwame sits with his jacket off and tie loosened, glasses on a cord, broad shoulders forward; his right hand flat on the desk mid-slap, his left resting gently on the pot of a white orchid. Behind him, a framed Accra Hearts of Oak scarf. The object that tells you who he is: a mug with the old Asante Freight logo, chipped. The wrong object: the orchid. Its leaves are yellowing and the soil is dark and wet. There is a ring of water on the desk under the pot. It is being loved to death.

## Room 7. The Fund (Dev Malhotra)

A glass meeting room on the 31st floor in the City at dusk. The light is the city itself, through floor-to-ceiling glass: cold indigo sky, a warm low strip of sunset behind the towers, and one recessed ceiling light over the table. Dominant colour: indigo, with the one gold of the sunset line. A long glass table reflecting everything. Dev sits very still in a grey suit without a tie, steel watch; both hands flat on the glass, symmetrical, unmoving, painted with care because they are the subject. Across from him, an empty chair where the player sits. The object that tells you who he is: a squash ball in a small dish beside his water glass. The wrong object: his phone lies face-down, but its edge is lit, and reflected in the glass table you can just see the wallpaper: a young woman in a graduation gown.

## Room 8. The Board (Richard Haldane and Fiona Blake)

A wood-panelled boardroom, early morning. The single light source is a tall window at the far end, behind Richard, so he is in silhouette with a rim of light on his shoulders and his face barely legible: Rembrandt's dark, almost. A long table, twelve chairs. Dominant colour: deep oxide red, the wood and the leather. Richard sits at the head, half-moon glasses, hands folded and still. Fiona sits to his right, sharp grey dress, glasses on her head, one hand flat on a closed folder. The object that tells you who he is: a small framed photograph of a puffin on the windowsill. The wrong object: at the far end of the table, nearest the player, one chair is pulled back at an angle, a glass of water in front of it half-drunk, still beaded. Someone left this room in a hurry minutes before you came in.

## Room 9. The Mirror

A grey room with no window. Two fluorescent tubes in a ceiling of acoustic tiles, one flickering on a slow cycle. Flat, shadowless, hospital light. Dominant colour: cold grey; the palette contains no warm pigment, by rule. A grey table. A single document on it, face-up, its text painted as illegible grey lines. A grey chair. On the far wall a full-length mirror in a plain steel frame. In the mirror, the player's figure: a dark silhouette, the same height as the player's chosen stance, no face, no features, edges painted soft as if out of focus. There are no objects that tell you who anyone is. That is the point. The wrong object: there is a pen on the table, uncapped, and its cap is nowhere in the room. On a second look, the cap is in the mirror, on the reflected table, and not on the real one.

## The War Room

A long map room in a Holborn law firm, 8:30 a.m., heavy rain. One pendant lamp hangs low over the table and has been knocked a few degrees off true, so every shadow falls slightly wrong. The single window, left, is grey and streaming. Dominant colour: arsenic green, the old baize and the walls, with craquelure in the map. Six chairs; one empty, pulled back, its cushion still dented. Five figures: Meera at the window, back half-turned; Daniel mid-pace; Priya seated, a tissue in each hand; Karan by the map, chalk in his knuckles; the doorway empty, waiting for Fatima. On the table, a glass; beneath it on the floor, a brass letter opener. The object that tells you who he was: his reading glasses, folded, at the empty chair. The wrong object: the map's pins and string. First look, a case board. Second look, the string traces the Thames estuary, and the pins mark a site no file covers.
