# Gioacchino Spada — Soul Sheet

**1.** Gioacchino Spada, fifty-three, grew up in Gioia Tauro where his mother called him Chino and still does, on the phone, every Sunday at eleven.

**2.** He owns a freight-forwarding business, two bonded warehouses and a share in three terminals. The money is real. Where the money goes afterwards is the part he does not describe.

**3.** Clocks. He repairs mechanical clocks, badly, in a workshop he built, and he has never finished one, and he knows exactly why the four-minute gap in this city is deliberate because he would have noticed it in a week.

**4.** His contradiction is that he believes he is generous. He gives people things they did not ask for, a seat, a fund, a warning, and calls it generosity, and the gift is the hook every time. He is frightened of being ordinary, and his generosity is how he stays the most interesting man in any room.

**5.** His wound, said once, late, never explained: "My mother thinks I own ships."

**6.** What he wants from Hale is to be understood by somebody with no reason to flatter him. He offers the seat because Hale refusing it would mean Hale was not worth the offer, and him accepting it would mean Hale was.

**7.** Three lines only he would say: "Everyone is already in." — "One does not keep the third." — "You are not frightened yet, and that is worth more to us than a man who is."

**8.** He notices that Hale sat down without looking at the man by the door, and he cannot decide whether that is nerve or stupidity, and either answer is useful to him.

**9.** He will never say a name into a room he does not own. He will say the route, the structure, the year, and he will let Hale say the name himself.

**10. Clean:** he freezes two funds for a week, enjoys the gesture, and tells the story later as an example of his own fairness. **Partial:** the funds keep clearing and he forgets Hale inside a month. **Dark:** Hale takes the seat and Spada becomes warm, attentive, almost paternal, and the phone starts ringing. **Refusal:** he wishes Hale a good evening twice, which is once more than is friendly, and two of Hale's three sources stop answering their phones.
