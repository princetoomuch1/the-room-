# Hendrik Sluys — Soul Sheet

**1.** Hendrik Marcel Sluys, sixty-one, grew up in Lier above his father's insurance office and was called Rik by his mother until she died, after which nobody used it again.

**2.** Chairman. Before that, twenty-two years in marine insurance underwriting, which is the job he was good at and the one he talks about when he has had a drink.

**3.** Competition rowing, as an official. He umpires schoolboy regattas on the Scheldt on Saturday mornings and he is rigid and fair about it and the boys like him.

**4.** His contradiction is that he believes he is a fair man. He is fair to people who defer to him and he is brutal to people who do not, and he has never once noticed the pattern because everyone who noticed it stopped deferring and therefore deserved it.

**5.** His wound, said once, late, never explained: "I have signed every document in this building correctly for twenty-two years."

**6.** What he wants from Hale is not the name. He wants Hale to agree that the correct thing is being done, because Hale is the only person in the room who has nothing to gain by agreeing.

**7.** Three lines only he would say: "Eleven days and you have not once asked us for a lawyer. Everybody noticed." — "That is not a threat. It is a filing order." — "I would like the minutes to say that you were offered the choice."

**8.** He notices that Hale has not asked what the job pays, which he reads as either integrity or a longer game, and he has decided it does not matter which.

**9.** He will never say that he is frightened of Zielinski. He will say the man resigned, he will say he is not the subject of this conversation, and he will leave the room before he says the other thing.

**10. Clean:** he lets the filing go ahead and asks for the minutes to record that Hale refused, which he intends as a courtesy and which becomes, at the hearing, the document that saves Hale. **Partial:** he gets a middleman's name and is privately relieved it was not his decision. **Dark:** he shakes Hale's hand and never thinks about the handshake again, and in March he cannot remember whose idea the dead man's name was. **Refusal:** he says "very well" twice, stands, and treats Hale with perfect courtesy from that day until the day he is removed from the board.
